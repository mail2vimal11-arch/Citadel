## 1. Size of the problem: fraud losses, breaches, data brokers, AI-enabled fraud

### Takeaway
Reported losses are at record highs in both countries (US FTC: $15.9B in 2025; Canada CAFC: CAD 704M in 2025, believed to capture only 5-10% of victims), and US breach volume set a record in 2025 and again in H1 2026. Identity-fraud losses proper (Javelin) are flat at about $27B, so the growth is in scams, account takeover and new-account fraud rather than in classic identity theft.

### Cited Findings

- FTC 2025 report counts as given in secondary coverage: over 6.2M total reports, over 3M fraud, nearly 1.4M identity theft (unverified against the 2025 Data Book, which did not surface) — [Crowdfund Insider](https://www.crowdfundinsider.com/2026/03/269398-us-ftc-reports-15-9-billion-in-consumer-fraud-losses-in-past-year/)

- Javelin's 2026 framing is that losses stabilised while AI-driven threats erode trust — [Nasdaq press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/fraud-losses-stabilize-ai-driven-threats-are-eroding-trust-2026-04-21)

### Inferences
- FTC-reported losses rose about 27% in 2025 ($12.5B to $15.9B) while Javelin's identity-fraud losses were flat. The headline growth is mostly scams (victim-authorised payments), which monitoring and data removal address only indirectly.
- If the CAFC's 5-10% reporting estimate also held for dollars, true Canadian losses would be on the order of CAD 7-14B a year. That scaling is crude (large losses are more likely to be reported) and should be presented as an order of magnitude only.
- Javelin's numbers imply an average identity-fraud loss of roughly $1,500 per victim ($27.3B / 18M), much of it absorbed by financial institutions rather than the consumer; this weakens the pure financial case for a $10-30/month product and shifts the value proposition toward time saved and peace of mind.
- 2025's 79% drop in victim notices followed by H1 2026 exceeding all of 2025 shows demand triggers are lumpy: acquisition is likely to spike after mega-breaches rather than grow smoothly.

- The FTC 2025 Consumer Sentinel Data Book itself and the ITRC H1 2026 report were not read directly.

- No credible study surfaced on the average number of companies holding one person's data.
- Details of 2024 mega-breaches (National Public Data, Change Healthcare) were not re-verified this session. From background knowledge, not checked: Change Healthcare was ultimately put at roughly 190M people and National Public Data at about 2.9B records claimed.
- WestJet and other 2025-26 Canadian incidents, and OPC breach-report counts, were not retrieved. A Global News item about a privacy-commissioner probe into a driver's-licence hack (around Sept 2026) appeared by title only — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12068230/privacy-commissioner-investigation-drivers-licence-hack).
- Voice-cloning-specific loss figures circulating online (for example a "1,210% surge") could not be traced to primary sources and should not be used.

## 2. Market size and the revenue of comparable businesses

### Takeaway
Publisher estimates of the "identity theft protection" market are unusable as a single number (2025 global estimates range from $4.6B to $17.6B). Company disclosures are firmer: Gen Digital is a $5B-revenue incumbent with about 79M paid customers, Aura reached about $238M ARR on 1.3M subscribers before listing on the ASX, Guardio claims $150M ARR on 1M payers, and pure data-removal players remain small or undisclosed.

### Inferences
- Blended revenue per subscriber: Aura about $183/year ($238M / 1.3M, roughly $15/month); Guardio about $150/year; Gen direct about $87/year ($7.25 x 12). Scaled consumer security businesses earn $7-15 per subscriber per month, below the $20-30 list prices of premium tiers.
- Qoria at about $675M on about $90M of trailing revenue is roughly 7.5x revenue (one data site says 7.7x), a useful public marker for a subscription safety business with institutional distribution.
- Data removal on its own has not yet produced a disclosed $100M-revenue company. The firms that reached scale bundle monitoring, insurance, device security or scam protection.
- Capital is concentrating in a few bets (Cloaked $375M, Guardio, Aura) while small specialists are being absorbed (Optery, Block Party, Ironwall).

## 3. Consumer demand, willingness to pay, churn and acquisition economics

### Takeaway
Stated concern is near-universal but paid adoption is a minority behaviour, and the one hard CAC datapoint (Aura: $171 CAC against a $129 first order) shows direct-to-consumer acquisition does not pay back in year one. The business only works with multi-year retention, which the incumbent achieves at about 78% a year.

- Low price floor exists: Consumer Reports found EasyOptOuts, at $19.99 a year, among the more effective removal services — [The Record](https://therecord.media/data-removal-services-mostly-worthless-study)

### Inferences
- Aura's CAC is 1.33x its first-order value, so a customer must renew at least once before acquisition cost is recovered, before any cost of service. A new entrant without Aura's scale should expect worse.
- At Gen's 78% retention the expected customer lifetime is about 4.5 years (1 / 0.22), or roughly $395 of lifetime revenue per direct customer at $87 a year. This is the benchmark a challenger's LTV must approach to afford $150+ CAC.
- Survey figures on willingness to pay (66-79%) overstate demand by a wide margin. No survey found gives a clean paid-penetration figure; a search summary's 15-25% range for US adults is an estimate, not a measured number.
- Annual prepay is the industry's standard defence against first-year churn; Aura's $129 order value suggests most direct customers buy annual plans.

### Gaps
- No category-specific data on free-to-paid conversion, first-year renewal for removal services, or CAC for Incogni, DeleteMe or Cloaked.
- A Recurly figure quoted in search results ("2-4% annual churn" for software) looks like a mislabelled monthly rate and was not used — [Recurly](https://www.recurly.com/research/churn-rate-benchmarks).
- No Pew Research result surfaced this session.
- OPC's exact figures for identity-theft concern, breach experience and protective actions (Figure 14 of the report) could not be read — [OPC report PDF](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2025/cpvp-opc/IP54-118-2025-eng.pdf).
- Family-plan uptake and price-elasticity data were not found.

## 4. Segments with above-average willingness to pay

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced high-value segments are people facing physical-safety risk from exposed addresses: crypto holders (wrench attacks up sharply in H1 2026), corporate executives (security perks up about 36% after the 2024 UnitedHealthcare shooting) and public officials. Seniors carry disproportionate losses but are usually reached through adult children or institutions.

### Cited Findings
- Crypto holders: CertiK counted 52 verified wrench attacks worldwide in H1 2026 (39 a year earlier) with about $124M in losses, roughly 12x H1 2025's $10.5M; publicly reported home invasions rose from 1 to 20 — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/374136/crypto-wrench-attacks-cost-victims-124m-in-six-months-up-12x-certik); [CertiK](https://www.certik.com/certik-report/intel3d/intel3d-wrench-h1-2026)
- Chainalysis, using a different method, documented 46 attacks and over $30M stolen through late June 2026, after a record $58M in 2025 — [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/375014/crypto-wrench-attacks-30m-stolen-2026-chainalysis)
- Researchers link the trend to leaked identifiable financial data that lets attackers find holders' homes; a January 2026 breach at crypto tax firm Waltio is cited — [The Record](https://therecord.media/wrench-attacks-against-cryptocurrency-holders); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/crypto-home-invasions-wrench-attacks-certik-h1-2026)
- Executives: median spending on security perks rose 36.3% from 2023 in early 2025 proxy filings — [Equilar](https://www.equilar.com/blogs/595-executive-security-spending-shifts.html)
- More than 34% of S&P 500 companies mentioned personal security measures as executive perks in 2025 proxies — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/securities-law/s-p-500-chiefs-turn-to-bodyguards-home-security-as-risks-grow)
- UnitedHealth disclosed nearly $1.7M of executive security spending for 2024; J&J and Eli Lilly raised theirs citing increased threats — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/unitedhealth-1-7-million-executive-security-disclosures/); [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2025/03/13/815385.htm)
- BlackCloak (digital executive protection) says its ARR nearly doubled in 2025 (vendor-reported, source page not confirmed) — [BlackCloak](https://blackcloak.io/?p=21734)
- Public officials: DeleteMe launched business-level plans for members of Congress in January 2025 after bomb threats and swatting incidents — [GlobeNewswire via Manila Times](https://manilatimes.net/2025/01/07/tmt-newswire/globenewswire/deleteme-announces-special-business-level-plans-to-protect-us-congress-and-house-politicians/2032516)
- Judges: New Jersey's Daniel's Law (2020), passed after the murder of a federal judge's son, bars online disclosure of covered officials' home addresses and phone numbers (described via a vendor page) — [DeleteMe business blog](https://joindeleteme.com/business/blog/data-broker-opt-outs-for-executive-cybersecurity-protection/)
- Seniors: adults 60+ bore 39.46% of FBI-recorded AI-fraud losses in 2025 on 14.05% of complaints, and reported about $3B in fraud losses to the FTC — [SecureWorld](https://www.secureworld.io/industry-news/ai-enabled-fraud-topped-893m-fbi); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/older-americans-lost-3-billion-to-fraud-in-2025-ftc-tells-congress/)
- Recent breach victims: Nova Scotia offered online protection to Cape Breton students and staff after a school cyberattack, showing breach victims usually receive monitoring free from the breached party — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7531415)

### Inferences
- Segments where the harm is physical (address exposure) rather than financial have clearer willingness to pay, and often an employer or institution pays. That makes them B2B or B2B2C sales more than consumer SaaS.
- Recent breach victims are a weak paying segment in the short run because they are handed 12-24 months of free monitoring; the opening is when that free cover lapses.
- Seniors' loss share suggests the adult-child buyer and family plans matter more than marketing to seniors directly.

### Gaps
- No pricing or deal-size data for executive-protection privacy contracts (BlackCloak, 360 Privacy, DeleteMe business).
- No evidence gathered on domestic-violence survivors, healthcare workers, journalists or small-business owners as paying segments.
- No Canadian data on any of these segments (for example Canadian crypto holders or judges).
- BlackCloak and 360 Privacy funding rounds were not retrieved.

## 5. B2B2C distribution

### Takeaway
Institutional channels are where incumbents win and where free tiers are appearing: U.S. Bank launched a free tier with automatic data-broker removal in October 2026. Wholesale breach-response pricing appears to be single-digit dollars per person per year, far below retail.

### Cited Findings
- Banks: U.S. Bank Protect 360 (launched 7 Oct 2026) offers a free Essentials tier for account holders including automatic removal from data broker sites and identity-theft recovery and reimbursement up to $1M; Premium is $9.99/month — [U.S. Bank](https://www.usbank.com/online-mobile-banking/protect-360.html); [FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/u-s-bank-expands-consumer-protection-tools-with-new-identity-privacy-and-credit--98d2ff3f); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-bank-joins-growing-list-of-banks-offering-in-demand-security-features)
- Chase offers free identity monitoring via Credit Journey but no premium identity product — [SafeHome](https://www.safehome.org/identity-theft-protection/chase/)
- Credit unions and fintech platforms distribute third-party products: SavvyMoney embedded Allstate Identity Protection restoration services; Ent FCU gave preferred members a free identity plan — [SavvyMoney](https://www.savvymoney.com/tag/allstate-identity-protection/); [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/creditunions/news/ent-fcu-provides-free-identity-theft-protection-to-preferred-members)
- Employers: a comparison site groups Norton LifeLock Benefit Solutions and Allstate Identity Protection as the full-service employer platforms, with Aura aimed at small and mid-size employers (no share data) — [FitGap](https://us.fitgap.com/search/employee-identity-theft-protection-software)
- An IdentityForce survey found 68% of HR professionals see identity-theft benefits growing in importance (sentiment, not adoption) — [HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/study-68-of-hr-managers-see-identity-theft-protection-as-a-vital-benefit/506209/)
- Norton sells employee-benefit identity plans in Canada with reimbursement cover up to CAD 1M on the top tier — [Norton Canada benefits checklist](https://www.nortonlifelock.com/content/dam/nortonlifelock/pdfs/partner/canada-employee-benefits-product-checklist.pdf)
- Breach response (unverified benchmark site): one-year basic monitoring is priced at roughly $4-8 per enrolled person when bundled with notification, versus $10-30 retail; settlements value monitoring at retail while the breached entity's cash cost is about $5-8 per enrollee per year — [Data breach cost profile](https://databreachcost.pages.dev/cost/credit-monitoring)
- Settlements commonly include two years of monitoring with up to $1M of insurance per person; a study of 305 settlements found a median $50 no-receipt cash payment — [Tech Insider on Fidelity](https://tech-insider.org/fidelity-data-breach-settlement-combined-fines-2026/); [OpenClassActions](https://openclassactions.com/blog/data-breach-settlement-payouts-per-person-study.php)
- Schools: Aura's Qoria deal adds about 32,000 school customers as an institutional channel to families — [Finance News Network](https://www.finnewsnetwork.com.au/archives/finance_news_network5463254.html)
- Insurers in Canada: Generali Global Assistance launched an identity-protection platform in Canada in 2017 and CyberScout sells through Canadian insurers to policyholders — [Canadian Underwriter](https://canadianunderwriter.ca/2017/06/07/generali-global-assistance-launches-identity-protection-platform-in-canada/); [Canadian Underwriter](https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/citb/cyberscout-brings-new-cyber-protection-offering-to-canadian-insurers-policyholders/)
- Telecom in Canada: TELUS Online Security is powered by Norton, with identity restoration and reimbursement tiers up to CAD 1M — [TELUS](https://www.telus.com/en/online-security/two-months-complete)

### Inferences
- A bank giving away data-broker removal is the clearest sign that basic removal is becoming a free feature of a financial relationship; a startup is more likely to be the white-label supplier to such programmes than to out-compete them at retail.
- At $4-8 per person per year wholesale, breach-response work is a volume business dominated by Experian, Kroll, IDX and similar firms; it suits a startup only as a lead source for upgrades.
- Partner revenue is material for incumbents (Gen: $429M in FY2025), confirming B2B2C as a core rather than side channel.

### Gaps
- No verified deal sizes for employer, insurer, bank or telecom contracts, and no measured employer adoption rate (vendor surveys only).

## 6. Canada

### Takeaway
Canada has record fraud losses, a thin product set dominated by the two credit bureaus and Norton (direct and via TELUS), and limited coverage from US privacy services. Credit freezes only became available in Ontario on 1 July 2026, and a federal anti-fraud agenda is still being built.

### Cited Findings
- Fraud scale: CAD 704M reported to the CAFC in 2025, with 5-10% reporting — [Investment Executive](https://www.investmentexecutive.com/industry-news/canadians-lost-704-million-to-fraud-in-2025/); [Competition Bureau](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2026/03/fraud-prevention-month-to-bring-hidden-crime-into-the-spotlight.html)
- Products: Equifax Complete Protection bundles credit monitoring, dark-web scanning, social media monitoring, device protection, parental controls, password manager and VPN — [Equifax Canada](https://www.equifax.ca/personal/identity-theft-protection/)
- A sponsored MoneySense page lists Equifax Complete Protection at CAD 34.95/month (unverified, sponsored content) — [MoneySense](https://www.moneysense.ca/save/fraud-and-scams/stolen-identity-is-it-too-late/)
- TransUnion Canada launched its myTrueIdentity product for organisations in 2019 — [TransUnion Canada](https://newsroom.transunion.ca/transunion-launches-identity-theft-protection-solution-as-cyber-fraud-continues-to-grow-in-canada/)
- Gaps in US services: Aura's advanced identity features are restricted to US customers, with only basic plans such as antivirus sold in Canada (third-party review) — [SafetyDetective](https://www.safetydetective.com/best-antivirus/aura)
- Removal services present: Optery is available to Canadian residents excluding Quebec; Incogni is available in Canada — [Optery](https://www.optery.com/optery-is-officially-available-for-residents-of-canada/); [Incogni blog](https://blog.incogni.com/?p=1741)

- Canadian media have long been sceptical of paid identity protection ("largely not worth the fees") — [Globe and Mail](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/globe-investor/personal-finance/household-finances/identity-theft-protection-services-largely-not-worth-the-fees-experts-say/article36504226/)
- Business-side cost: a TransUnion Canada study reports 26% of business leaders attributing fraud losses to synthetic identity fraud — [Insurance Business Canada](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/ca/news/cyber/canadian-businesses-face-111-billion-in-fraud-losses-as-synthetic-identity-scams-surge--transunion-552447.aspx)

### Inferences
- No Canadian market-size figure exists. Scaling IBISWorld's US $5.7B by relative population (roughly one-eighth; population figures are an assumption, not sourced this session) gives a ceiling near $0.7B, and actual Canadian spend is probably well below that given fewer products and long-standing consumer-advocate scepticism.
- No Canadian-founded consumer privacy or data-removal startup surfaced in searches. That is either a real gap or a sign the market is too small to have attracted one; the evidence does not distinguish.
- Ontario's new free credit freeze removes one reason to buy monitoring, but also gives a guardian product a concrete free action to automate for users.
- Quebec's exclusion from Optery's Canadian launch suggests Law 25 and French-language requirements add cost for entrants; a Canadian-built product could treat Quebec as a differentiator.
- Canada has no equivalent of California's Delete Act or a broker registry, so automated removal in Canada rests on PIPEDA access and deletion requests to each company.

### Gaps
- Bank-led offerings from RBC, TD, BMO, Scotiabank or CIBC, DeleteMe's Canadian availability, and current TELUS pricing were not retrieved.
- Status of the Financial Crimes Agency legislation and of federal privacy-law reform as of Nov 2026 is unconfirmed.
- Quebec's freeze start date conflicts across sources (2023 in most; one says provisions took effect July 2026).
- OPC breach-report statistics and Canadian subscriber counts for any identity product were not found.

## 7. Risks and headwinds

### Takeaway
The three biggest headwinds are free substitutes from governments and banks (California DROP, U.S. Bank's free removal tier), independent evidence that removal services underperform, and acquisition costs that exceed first-year revenue. Big-tech bundling is real but uneven: Google shut down its dark web report in early 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Effectiveness: Consumer Reports (Aug 2024, with Tall Poppy; 32 volunteers, seven services, 13 people-search sites, four months) found manual opt-outs removed 70% of profiles within a week versus 0% to 58.7% for paid services; the weakest were Confidently (4%) and ReputationDefender (6%). Authors say results are not statistically significant — [Consumer Reports Innovation Lab](https://innovation.consumerreports.org/new-report-data-defense-evaluating-people-search-site-removal-services/); [The Record](https://therecord.media/data-removal-services-mostly-worthless-study)
- Consumer Reports also found some removal services advertise on or partner with people-search sites — [Consumer Reports advocacy release](https://advocacy.consumerreports.org/press_release/consumer-reports-evaluation-of-people-search-site-removal-services-finds-that-they-are-largely-ineffective/)
- Optery disputes the study design and cites a 68% removal rate against 70% for manual opt-outs — [Optery statement](https://www.optery.com/optery-statement-on-consumer-reports-people-search-removal-study/)
- Government substitute: California's DROP opened 1 Jan 2026 as a free, single-request deletion tool covering all registered brokers; brokers had to start processing on 1 Aug 2026 and re-check at least every 45 days. It is limited to California residents and no other state has an equivalent — [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/economy/technology/2026/01/californians-block-personal-data/); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/08/californians-can-tell-data-brokers-to-drop-their-information)
- Bank substitute: U.S. Bank's free tier includes automatic data-broker removal — [U.S. Bank](https://www.usbank.com/online-mobile-banking/protect-360.html)
- Free credit-bureau membership at scale: Experian has over 215M free members — [Experian](https://www.experian.com/blogs/news/2026/05/19/fy26-experian-leading-next-era-of-ai-enabled-growth/)
- Big tech is not uniformly advancing: Google ended dark web report scans on 15 Jan 2026 and shut the tool on 16 Feb 2026, saying it did not give users helpful next steps — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/15/googles-dark-web-report-feature-will-no-longer-be-available-starting-in-february/); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/12/15/google-dark-web-report-shutting-down/)
- Authorised-agent friction: a Consumer Reports test with over 100 volunteers found companies unprepared for agent-submitted deletion requests; a law-firm alert says California's Attorney General alleged some businesses failed to honour such requests and flags identity-verification concerns — [Scripps News](https://www.turnto23.com/news/national/authorized-agent-apps-will-help-you-delete-your-online-data); [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/news-insights/2026-will-be-the-year-of-the-authorized-agent.html)
- Unit-economics risk: Aura's $171 CAC exceeds its $129 first order — [Merger update, 26 Mar 2026](https://investorpa.com/announcement/273629/)
- Aura narrowed losses mainly by cutting cost and marketing ($55M programme) — [Finance News Network](https://www.finnewsnetwork.com.au/archives/finance_news_network5463254.html)
- Breach fatigue and low transparency: 70% of 2025 US breach notices omitted attack details — [Insurance Journal](https://amp.insurancejournal.com/news/national/2026/02/18/858534.htm)

### Inferences
- Google's stated reason for closing its dark web report (alerts without next steps) is direct support for a product that acts on the user's behalf rather than only monitoring.
- "Removal from people-search sites" is commoditising toward free; defensibility has to come from breadth (all companies holding data, not just brokers), verified outcomes, or a trusted distribution partner.
- The trust barrier is structural: a guardian must itself hold a full identity profile, and Consumer Reports' finding of ties between removal services and people-search sites gives sceptics a ready argument.

### Gaps
- No evidence gathered this session on Apple, Microsoft or password-manager bundling, on any 2026 expansion of Google's "Results about you", or on big-tech AI assistants acting as privacy agents.
- No measured data on first-year churn for removal services.
- No follow-up effectiveness study after 2024 was found.

## 8. Investor and acquirer appetite, and exit paths

### Takeaway
Appetite in 2025-26 is real but concentrated: large cheques went to a few scaled or heavily backed players, and the observed exits for small privacy specialists are undisclosed-price sales to consumer-security groups. Public-market reception of the one new listing (Aura) appears to have been weak.

### Cited Findings
- Cloaked raised $375M in March 2026 (General Catalyst, Liberty City Ventures), with part structured as growth financing from a customer-acquisition fund — [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/privacy-platform-cloaked-raises-375m-to-expand-consumer-tools-and-enterprise-reach/amp/)

- Aura: $140M Series G in March 2025, then a public listing via the Qoria scrip merger at about $2.1B combined pre-money, below its 2021 private mark of $2.5B for Aura alone — [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/aura-140-million-series-g-raised-for-ai-based-safety-solutions); [Aura press release](https://www.aura.com/press/release/aura-qoria-acquisition-announcement); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/consumer-security-firm-aura-raises-200-million-25-billion-valuation/amp/)
- Strategic roll-ups of small specialists: Surfshark took a majority of Optery (Sept 2026) after Ironwall (2024); DeleteMe bought Block Party (March 2026); all prices undisclosed — [Surfshark](https://surfshark.com/blog/surfshark-acquires-optery); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/25/deleteme-acquires-social-media-security-tool-block-party)
- Incumbent diversification: Gen bought MoneyLion for about $1B, moving toward financial wellness — [Finovate](https://finovate.com/cyber-safety-company-gen-digital-acquires-moneylion-for-1-billion/)
- Private equity as buyer: Kingswood Capital acquired IDX from ZeroFox (Nov 2024) — [PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/x7SxVA)

### Inferences
- Likely exit paths for a consumer privacy startup, in order of observed frequency: (1) acquisition by a consumer-security or VPN group (Nord/Surfshark, Gen, Aura, DeleteMe, Malwarebytes-type buyers), (2) private equity, (3) credit bureau or insurer. IPO has only been reached at $200M+ ARR and via merger.
- Optery sold a majority after raising under $10M and posting fast growth, which suggests founders of capital-efficient removal businesses are choosing strategic sales over further venture rounds.
- Cloaked's use of customer-acquisition financing shows investors treat this as a paid-marketing-driven category where capital funds CAC rather than R&D; an under-capitalised entrant is at a disadvantage in paid channels.
- A Canadian founder is more likely to raise on a differentiated wedge (Canada/Quebec compliance, B2B2C partner contracts, agentic automation beyond brokers) than on a general consumer removal product.

### Gaps
- No verified data on Aura's post-listing share-price performance (one search summary said the stock "more than halved" since the deal was announced; source page not identified).
- No disclosed prices for Optery, Block Party, Ironwall or IDX.
- No aggregate figure for venture funding into consumer privacy in 2024-26, and no Canadian investor activity in the category, was found.
- Valuations for Cloaked, DeleteMe, Proton and 1Password in 2025-26 were not retrieved.
