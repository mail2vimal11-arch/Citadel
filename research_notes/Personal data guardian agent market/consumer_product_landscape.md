# D2C product landscape: services that protect, monitor and manage an individual's personal data (as of 12 Nov 2026)

Method note (read first). Research date: 12 Nov 2026. Full-page fetches failed for every host tried this session (www.cloaked.com, www.businesswire.com, innovation.consumerreports.org did not resolve under the session's network settings), so **every cited finding below comes from web-search result summaries/snippets, not from reading the full page**. Prices in particular conflict between sources (promotions, live price tests) and should be re-checked on vendor pages. Anything drawn from my own background knowledge (training cutoff mid-2026) rather than a source found this session is placed under "Gaps" and labelled BACKGROUND. Many 2026 comparison sites surfaced by search (offlist.me, crabclear, privacyon, halocard, securityhero, costbench) are competitors or affiliate sites; they are flagged where used.

## 1. For each product: which of protect / monitor / manage does it do? Per-company ledger? Leak attribution? Legal requests beyond data brokers?

### Takeaway
The market is split into three silos that rarely overlap: alias/masking tools (protect), breach/credit monitoring suites (monitor), and data-broker removal services (a narrow slice of manage). Only a handful of products touch "ordinary companies" (Cloaked Actions, Permission Slip/DeleteMe, Mine/McAfee Online Account Cleanup, Privacy Bee, Rightly, Yorba, PrivacyHawk), and none found this session markets automated attribution of a leak to a specific company with a timestamp; alias vendors only say aliases make it "easier to identify" the source.

### Inferences
- "Per-company ledger" exists only in the inbox-scan family (Mine/McAfee Online Account Cleanup, Cloaked Actions via Gmail, Rightly Protect, Yorba) and, implicitly, in alias managers (each alias row = one company). Monitoring suites and broker-removal services keep a ledger of brokers, not of the user's actual counterparties.
- No product found fuses the two ledgers (aliases issued per company + inbox-discovered legacy accounts) into one record, and none joins breach/broker/spam signals back to a specific alias to say "Company X leaked or sold this on date Y". Cloaked has both raw ingredients in one app but markets attribution only as something the user can infer.
- "Manage" for ordinary companies is thin everywhere: mostly templated email requests with weak follow-through; consent withdrawal and access (DSAR) requests are rarely productised compared with deletion/opt-out.

### Gaps
- BACKGROUND (not verified this session): Fastmail has masked email natively integrated with 1Password (since 2021) and Bitwarden; 1Password also integrated Privacy.com virtual cards (US). Firefox Relay phone masking is US/Canada only. Apple Hide My Email is available worldwide incl. Canada. Bitdefender DIP and HIBP are available in Canada.
- Could not confirm whether Proton Pass, SimpleLogin, DuckDuckGo Email Protection or Apple label a breach hit by the specific alias/company in-product (search found no documentation of a "who leaked" feature).
- Could not verify how Cloaked "Actions" sends requests (authorized-agent email, web forms, API) or its completion rate.
- No current detail found on what McAfee did with SayMine after closing, or whether saymine.com still accepts new users.
- Equifax/TransUnion/LifeLock feature detail is thin; see section 2.

## 2. Pricing tiers and business model; countries supported; availability to Canadians

### Takeaway
Almost everything is a US$8–30/month consumer subscription (annual billing pushed hard); free tiers exist only for aliases, breach lookup and nonprofit/open-source request tools. Canada is a second-class market: aliases and broker removal are available (Cloaked, Incogni, Optery, DeleteMe, MySudo numbers, Proton, Apple), but virtual cards (Privacy.com, MySudo, IronVest, Revolut) and most identity-theft suites (NordProtect, DuckDuckGo removal, Aura unconfirmed) are US-only or unverified for Canada.

## 4. Notable failures, shutdowns, pivots and criticisms, and what they teach

### Takeaway
The category's history is consolidation and retreat by non-specialists: Mozilla, Google, Jumbo, Mine and Consumer Reports all exited or handed off consumer privacy products in 2023–2026, while specialists (DeleteMe, Surfshark, Cloaked, McAfee) absorbed them. The recurring lessons are that monitoring without next steps is not valued, removal efficacy is weak and hard to verify, trust/conflict-of-interest scandals are fatal to partnerships, and free "request agents" do not sustain themselves.

### Cited Findings
- Consumer Reports study (Aug 2024): 32 volunteers (California, New York), 7 removal services, 13 people-search sites, four months; services "largely ineffective as a whole"; best performers EasyOptOuts and Optery at roughly 65–68% removal after four months (Optery claims 68% and first place; The Record's phrasing gives the reverse order), manual opt-outs ~70%; prices ranged US$19.99–249/yr; authors noted results are not statistically significant — [The Record](https://therecord.media/data-removal-services-mostly-worthless-study); [Optery statement (vendor)](https://www.optery.com/optery-statement-on-consumer-reports-people-search-removal-study/); [Privacy Guides forum](https://discuss.privacyguides.net/t/consumer-reports-evaluating-people-search-site-removal-services/19948)
- Reporting around the study also flagged financial partnerships between people-search sites and removal services — [Marketplace Tech](https://www.marketplace.org/shows/marketplace-tech/do-paid-data-removal-services-pay-off/)
- Onerep: Krebs (14 Mar 2024) showed CEO Dimitri Shelest had launched dozens of people-search services and co-founded broker Nuwber; Mozilla said it would drop Onerep in Mar 2024 but was still using it 16+ months later, then shut Monitor Plus entirely (ended 17 Dec 2025), saying it could not meet its "expectations for value and reliability" with available vendors — [Krebs 2024](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2024/03/mozilla-drops-onerep-after-ceo-admits-to-running-people-search-networks/); [Krebs Nov 2025](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2025/11/mozilla-says-its-finally-done-with-two-faced-onerep); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/mozilla-ditches-privacy-partner-over-conflict-of-interest)
- Google ended its dark web report (Jan–Feb 2026) because it "didn't provide helpful next steps"; steered users to passkeys/Password Manager — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/12/15/google-dark-web-report-shutting-down/); [BGR](https://bgr.com/2053246/google-discontinued-dark-web-tool-checks-personal-data-why)
- Jumbo Privacy: acqui-hired by cyber-insurer Coalition (Jul 2023); app no longer updated, features stripped, App Store listing ended Jun 2024 — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/12/cyber-insurance-startup-coalition-acquires-privacy-assistant-jumbo); [Jumbo](https://blog.jumboprivacy.com/jumbo_wind_down)
- Mine: after raising venture money on a consumer "find who has your data" app (100,000+ users and 1.3M requests by Oct 2020), pivoted to enterprise (MineOS) and sold the consumer app to McAfee in Nov 2025 "to focus entirely on our enterprise mission" — [TechCrunch 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/10/21/mine-series-a/amp/); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/mineos-mcafee/amp/)

- CR's authorized-agent tests found companies "aren't really ready to deal with these requests"; CR launched a Data Rights Protocol to standardise agent requests — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/privacy/authorized-agents-plan-to-make-it-easier-to-delete-your-data-a8655835448/); [Privacy Daily](https://privacy-daily.com/article/2025/01/27/consumer-reports-data-rights-protocol-aims-to-standardize-agent-requests-2501270014)
- A 2026 academic study of California-registered brokers found partial compliance, significant non-response and CCPA-disallowed identity-verification demands; some brokers require government ID to process opt-outs — [arXiv 2607.04552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04552); [Bloomberg Government](https://news.bgov.com/states-of-play/california-brokers-not-honoring-delete-requests-states-of-play)
- IronVest's masked cards paused for 1–2 years over banking infrastructure; masked-email reliability complaints — [PrivacyOn](https://www.privacyon.com/blog/is-ironvest-worth-it-in-2026)
- Incogni custom removals reported by users as taking months or never finishing even when marked resolved (reported by a competitor) — [Aura](https://www.aura.com/learn/is-incogni-worth-it)
- Aura disclosed "limited customer information exposure" in March 2026 (a privacy vendor is itself a breach target) — [Security Hero (third-party review)](https://www.securityhero.io/identity-theft-protection/is-aura-legit-or-a-scam/)
- Agent trust risk: "Instinct", a 2026 AI assistant, drew criticism for indexing/retaining email data and initially being unable to delete a user's Gmail records on request — [TechCrunch, Aug 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/24/instincts-powerful-ai-assistant-is-raising-privacy-and-security-concerns/)
- Government substitution: California's free DROP platform (live 1 Jan 2026; brokers must process from 1 Aug 2026 and re-check every 45 days) lets residents delete from all ~580–600+ registered brokers in one request; 300,000+ sign-ups by June and ~345,000 by August 2026 — [CalPrivacy](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/06/privacy-momentum-builds-300000-californians-sign-up-for-drop-as-registered-data-brokers-hit-a-record-high/); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/calprivacy-unpacks-drop-updates-on-consumer-participation-upcoming-enforcement)

### Inferences
- Lesson 1 (monitoring alone is a feature, not a product): Google's stated reason for killing dark web report is direct evidence that alerts without remediation do not retain users; the guardian concept's "rotate and request" loop addresses exactly this.
- Lesson 2 (efficacy and verifiability): ~65–70% best-case removal and unverifiable "blanket" requests (Privacy Bee) mean outcome proof (confirmations, re-scan evidence, attribution) is a differentiator.
- Lesson 3 (trust is the product): Onerep's conflict of interest and the Instinct and Aura incidents show an agent holding inbox access and identity data must have clean ownership, minimal retention and auditable behaviour.
- Lesson 4 (requests to ordinary companies are operationally hard and poorly monetised): Mine and CR both built it, neither sustained it standalone; both ended inside larger subscription bundles.
- Lesson 5 (third-party dependency): stand-in infrastructure depends on banks/telcos (IronVest card pause; Cloaked Pay still gated; cards US-only everywhere).
- Lesson 6 (regulatory substitution): free state tools like DROP commoditise broker deletion in California, pushing paid services toward non-broker value.

### Gaps
- BACKGROUND (CR 2024 study detail, unverified this session): overall only ~35% of profiles were removed within four months across services; Confidently and ReputationDefender were among the worst performers.
- Could not retrieve the "lessons" section of CR's two-year reflections post or the terms/price of the DeleteMe transfer.
- No verified data on how often ordinary companies honour agent-submitted deletion requests in 2025–2026.
- Did not find documented criticism/billing complaints specific to Cloaked this session (not searched in depth).

### Inferences
- The "verified attribute proof" leg of the guardian concept is being supplied by platform wallets (Apple, Google, national eID), not by privacy apps; a guardian agent would most plausibly orchestrate those wallets rather than issue credentials itself.
- The absence of a funded pure-play "personal data agent" start-up in search results, combined with Cloaked's explicit roadmap, suggests the window is open but the first well-capitalised mover is already declared.
- Enterprise DSR automation (DataGrail, MineOS) means the receiving side of requests is becoming machine-readable, which should make consumer-side agent requests cheaper over time (CR's Data Rights Protocol points the same way).
