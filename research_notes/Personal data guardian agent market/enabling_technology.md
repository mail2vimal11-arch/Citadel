## 1. Email aliasing / masking (SimpleLogin, addy.io, Apple Hide My Email, Fastmail Masked Email; deliverability; blocking; open source)

### Takeaway
Per-company email aliases are the most mature, cheapest and most Canada-available building block: open-source, self-hostable code exists (SimpleLogin, addy.io) and retail pricing is roughly US$1-5 per user per month. The real limits are operational, not technical: some sites reject known alias domains, forwarding/reply paths occasionally break, and aliases complicate account recovery.

### Cited Findings
- SimpleLogin is owned by Proton, and Proton Pass's built-in aliases run on SimpleLogin — [iPFS/anoni.net alias guide](https://ipfs.anoni.net/en/tools/email-alias/)
- SimpleLogin states that all its components (web app, iOS/Android apps, browser extensions) are available on GitHub; the same post says it found no self-hosting instructions for Firefox Relay — [SimpleLogin blog](https://simplelogin.io/blog/vs-firefox-relay/)
- addy.io (formerly AnonAddy) is described by third-party comparison guides as open source and self-hostable (exact page behind this snippet not confirmed) — [Privacy tools comparison 2026](https://theluckystrike.github.io/privacy-tools-guide/articles/privacy-focused-email-alias-services-comparison-2026); [DEV Community](https://dev.to/ahsanluqman/the-best-anonaddy-alternatives-46k7)
- Free tiers: addy.io free plan includes 10 shared-domain aliases — [Privacy Guides](https://direct.privacyguides.org/en/email-aliasing); SimpleLogin free tier supports only ten aliases — [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/best-email-alias-services-2026-3685703/)
- Paid pricing (third-party comparison sites, Oct 2026, conflicting; verify on official pages): addy.io Lite about US$1/month billed yearly, a "Standard" tier at US$3.99/month; SimpleLogin Premium quoted at US$3-5/month or US$4/month — [emcognito comparison](https://emcognito.com/compare); [alejandrorioja.com comparison](https://alejandrorioja.com/email-alias-services-compared/)
- Fastmail Masked Email has no free tier — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/ahsanluqman/the-best-anonaddy-alternatives-46k7); masked addresses default to the fastmail.com domain but a custom domain can be used — [macwright.org](https://macwright.org/2023/09/24/masked-email)
- Password managers already integrate alias generation: Bitwarden has integrations with Fastmail, SimpleLogin, addy.io and others — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/708303/what-is-masked-email-this-new-spin-on-an-old-practice-supercharges-security.html)
- Apple runs two distinct systems: "Sign in with Apple" creates per-app relay addresses at privaterelay.appleid.com, while "Hide My Email" is an iCloud+ feature producing addresses on icloud.com — [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2023/08/private-relay-vs-private-relay-vs-hide.html)
- Apple relay addresses are forward-only, and senders must register their sending domains with Apple and authenticate with SPF/DKIM, or mail to privaterelay.appleid.com recipients is rejected — [Resend docs](https://resend.com/docs/knowledge-base/sending-apple-private-relay); [Customer.io docs](https://docs.customer.io/journeys/authenticating-for-apple-private-relay/)
- A common bounce cause for Apple relay addresses is the user having deleted the Hide My Email address — [VirtualBadge help](https://help.virtualbadge.io/article/apple-emails-not-updating-status-after-email-sending)
- Blocking of alias domains is real but only anecdotally documented: a Reddit user reported a major news site accepted a Fastmail masked address but rejected SimpleLogin aliases, including on a custom domain — [r/Simplelogin mirror](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/Simplelogin/comments/1iba1nq/simplelogin_less_and_less_usable); a Privacy Guides forum thread is devoted to what to do when a service will not accept addy.io or SimpleLogin addresses — [Privacy Guides forum](https://discuss.privacyguides.net/t/when-a-service-wont-accept-your-addy-io-or-simplelogin-email-what-do-you-use/21436/13)
- Reply-path failure example: a user reported replies sent to a SimpleLogin alias bounced because the envelope sender did not match their real address — [HEY World blog](https://world.hey.com/valentyn/how-my-email-service-failed-me-and-why-it-matters-for-privacy-9465b69a)
- Brian Krebs notes that not all websites allow aliases, that they can complicate account recovery, and (reader comment) that some services "de-alias" plus-addresses by stripping the +tag — [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2022/08/the-security-pros-and-cons-of-using-email-aliases/)
- Firefox Relay phone masking is reported as available in the US and Canada only; Relay Premium pricing is reported inconsistently (US$0.99/month vs. a waitlist with no price shown) — [emcognito comparison](https://emcognito.com/compare); [Privacy tools comparison 2026](https://theluckystrike.github.io/privacy-tools-guide/articles/privacy-focused-email-alias-services-comparison-2026)

### Inferences
- Nothing about email aliasing is geography-restricted; a Canadian startup can self-host open-source alias software or run its own forwarding on commodity mail infrastructure. This is the one component that is fully "feasible today" in Canada.
- The blocking problem is structural: any alias domain shared across many users becomes a recognisable "alias provider" domain and lands on blocklists, while a unique custom domain per user avoids blocklists but turns the domain itself into a cross-company identifier that defeats unlinkability. Expect to manage a rotating pool of ordinary-looking domains. (Inference; no quantitative blocking rate was found.)
- Apple Hide My Email and Fastmail are end-user features, not wholesale APIs a startup can build on; the build-on options are SimpleLogin/addy.io (API or self-host) or own infrastructure.
- An alias forwarder necessarily holds the alias-to-real-address map and sees message flow, so the guardian becomes a mail intermediary with the associated security and lawful-access exposure.

### Gaps
- No measured rate of how many sites reject alias domains; all evidence is forum anecdote.
- Official pricing/API pages for SimpleLogin, addy.io and Fastmail were not fetched; prices above are from comparison sites.
- A single unconfirmed source reported Apple planning to move new Sign in with Apple relay addresses to private.icloud.com later in 2026; not verified.
- Technical detail on forwarding breakage (SPF failure on forward, reliance on DKIM/ARC/SRS) is background knowledge, not verified this session.

## 2. Virtual phone numbers (Twilio, Telnyx, Bandwidth; cost; VoIP rejection for verification codes; SIM-swap protection)

### Takeaway
Programmable numbers are cheap (about US$1 per number per month wholesale) and available in Canada, but they are VoIP-classified, and banks, big platforms and identity-verification services commonly refuse to send verification codes to VoIP numbers. A unique number per company is therefore both economically and functionally impractical; phone masking works for low-stakes contact fields, not as the 2FA number for important accounts.

### Cited Findings
- Telnyx lists numbers at US$1.00 per number per month, with volume tiers (e.g., next 200 numbers at US$0.79/month); this is a general list price, not confirmed Canada-specific — [Telnyx pricing](https://telnyx.com/pricing/numbers.md)
- Twilio's Canada pricing page lists US$1.15/month for long-code numbers and US$2.15/month for toll-free (column attribution in the snippet was not fully clear) — [Twilio Canada SMS pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/sms/pricing/ca)
- Services known to restrict VoIP numbers for verification include Google Workspace/Google Accounts, Microsoft, banks, payment providers and identity-verification platforms; there is no universal list because each provider sets its own policy — [Zuper docs](https://docs.zuper.co/Zuper_Connect/OTP/2FA%20Verification%20Codes); [CloudTalk help](https://help.cloudtalk.io/en/articles/11403518-limitations-receiving-otp-and-2fa-sms-messages)
- Financial institutions prefer numbers tied directly to major carriers — [CurrentClient](https://www.currentclient.com/learn/troubleshooting/2fa-voip)
- Twitter/X stopped accepting Google Voice numbers for SMS 2FA in December 2022 — [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2022/12/21/twitter-2fa-google-voice-support/); Indeed no longer allows non-fixed VoIP numbers for account verification — [Indeed support](https://support.indeed.com/hc/en-nz/articles/34540805299085)
- Canada SIM-swap/port-out context: carriers adopted an industry-wide port-request verification process in 2020 — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2020/11/05/canadian-carriers-implement-new-number-porting-verification-process-to-prevent-fraud/); the CRTC had logged 24,627 likely cases of port and SIM-swap fraud in a 10-month period — [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2021/09/30/crtc-logged-24627-likely-cases-of-port-and-sim-swap-fraud-in-10-months/)
- The carriers' association says the 2020 measures produced a 95% decline in unauthorized transfers and SIM-related fraud over six months; advocates want a public CRTC consultation on port protection, which the CWTA opposed — [Yahoo News Canada](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/crtc-should-open-public-consultations-to-update-existing-number-port-protection-industry-experts-143838064.html)
- The CRTC and carriers have declined to publish SIM-swap statistics, citing confidentiality — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/regulators-and-telecoms-are-refusing-to-release-data-about-sim-swapping-in-canada)
- CRTC Telecom Decision 2026-185 (24 July 2026) deals with number-portability provisioning timing and states the change would help maintain fraud-prevention measures (only fragments seen) — [CRTC](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/archive/2026/2026-185.htm)
- A 2025 CBC case documented a Canadian customer's number being given to another customer without consent — [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/nova-scotia/cellphone-number-sim-swapping-security-risks-1.7456092)

### Inferences
- At roughly US$1/number/month wholesale, a truly unique number per company (dozens to hundreds per user) would cost more than a plausible consumer subscription. Realistic designs use one or a few masked numbers per user, or a shared pool with routing. Phone-based leak attribution is therefore much coarser than email-based attribution.
- A VoIP number controlled by the guardian is not exposed to carrier SIM-swap, but it relocates account-takeover risk to the guardian's own account security and its telecom provider; it does not eliminate it.
- "Rotate a compromised phone number" is feasible for the masked number, but every account using it for 2FA must then be updated, and many high-value accounts will not have accepted it in the first place.

### Gaps
- Bandwidth pricing and Canadian number inventory were not retrieved.
- Canadian regulatory obligations for a startup reselling numbers (CRTC reseller registration, 9-1-1, anti-spam/CASL for messaging) were not researched.
- No data on what share of Canadian banks/services reject VoIP numbers; evidence is vendor help-centre pages, mostly US-centric.
- Whether carrier-grade (non-VoIP) numbers via MVNO/eSIM partnerships are obtainable at startup scale in Canada, and at what cost, was not researched.

## 3. Virtual / disposable payment cards (Stripe Issuing, Marqeta, Lithic/Privacy.com, Adyen; regulation; Canada availability; merchant-locked cards)

### Takeaway
This is the weakest building block for a Canada-based founder. Merchant-locked and single-use consumer cards exist in the US (Privacy.com) but no equivalent for Canadian consumers was found; Stripe Issuing in Canada is a business-only preview; and issuing your own cards means a BIN-sponsor bank, per-user KYC, and dual FINTRAC MSB plus Bank of Canada RPAA registration.

### Cited Findings
- Privacy.com offers merchant-locked cards (usable only at one merchant) and single-use cards (close after one payment), and its issuing infrastructure became Lithic — [Privacy.com blog](https://www.privacy.com/blog/our-journey-from-privacy-com-to-lithic); [Privacy.com virtual cards](https://privacy.com/virtual-cards)
- Privacy.com is available only to US residents who link a US bank account; international users are locked out — [Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/privacy-dot-com-alternatives); [Cloaked](https://www.cloaked.com/post/privacy-com-alternatives)
- Stripe has published Canadian Issuing accountholder terms labelled "Preview" (last updated 2 July 2026), with cards issued by Stripe Payments Canada Limited; the terms have the user confirm it acts as a business enterprise and not as a consumer — [Stripe Canada charge-card terms](https://stripe.com/legal/issuing/charge-card-accountholder-terms-ca); [Stripe Canada prepaid terms](https://stripe.com/legal/issuing/prepaid-accountholder-terms-ca)
- A Stripe customer story says Emburse will be among the first to offer Stripe-powered cards to Canadian businesses "when Issuing launches in Canada in 2026" (forward-looking statement by Stripe) — [Stripe/Emburse](https://stripe.com/customers/emburse)
- Stripe's consumer issuing documentation says it is available only in the United States, and consumer prepaid debit cards are a developer preview requiring sales contact — [Stripe consumer issuing docs](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/consumer-issuing); [Stripe consumer prepaid docs](https://docs.stripe.com/issuing/consumer-prepaid-debit-cards)
- The standard route to issuing is a sponsor bank that provides its BIN and regulatory coverage — [Stripe global issuance guide](https://stripe.com/at/resources/more/global-card-issuance-strategies)
- Canadian BIN sponsors that market programs to fintechs: Peoples Group (sponsored network BIN, settlement, program oversight, virtual and physical cards) — [Peoples Group](https://www.peoplesgroup.com/card-issuing); Equitable Bank (BIN sponsorship for prepaid, credit, secured and multi-currency cards) — [Equitable Bank](https://www.equitablebank.ca/payment-as-a-service)
- Since 8 September 2025, payment service providers in Canada generally need both FINTRAC MSB registration (AML) and Bank of Canada registration under the Retail Payment Activities Act (operational risk, fund safeguarding, incident reporting); prepaid card programs are named among businesses that may need to register (secondary source) — [Tazapay](https://tazapay.com/en-sg/blog/canadas-dual-registration-for-payment-companies-fintrac-msb-rpaa-explained)
- The first RPAA annual report was due 31 March 2026, and as of 23 March 2026 the Bank of Canada listed 32 businesses as refused or revoked — [McCarthy Tetrault](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/techlex/psps-reminder-annual-report-due-in-one-week-other-rpaa-related-updates)
- A consultancy page puts the Bank of Canada registration fee at C$2,500 (not an official source) — [faisalkhan.com](https://faisalkhan.com/solutions/licensing/country/canada/rpaa)
- Canadian consumer virtual cards that exist today: KOHO virtual prepaid Mastercard with in-app lock/unlock — [KOHO](https://www.koho.ca/learn/best-virtual-cards-with-spending-controls-and-limits/); Wealthsimple prepaid Mastercard with virtual and physical card, and EQ Bank Card (prepaid Mastercard requiring an EQ account) — [Loans Canada](https://loanscanada.ca/money/10-questions-to-ask-about-prepaid-cards); Neo offers a real credit card rather than only prepaid — [Hardbacon](https://hardbacon.ca/en/credit-cards/best-virtual-credit-card/)
- Revolut is not available in Canada — [Finder Canada](https://www.finder.com/ca/banking/debit-cards/best-virtual-debit-cards-canada); it withdrew in 2021, appointed Jan Pilbauer to lead Revolut Canada in 2025, and as of August 2026 had announced no launch date and no public Canadian bank-licence application — [NCFA Canada](https://ncfacanada.org/revolut-company-profile-french-bank-canada/)
- In Canada, single-use and merchant-restricted virtual cards are marketed mainly by business spend-management fintechs (Float, Venn, Airwallex); no consumer bank was found confirming merchant-locked cards — [Float](https://floatfinancial.com/blog/virtual-credit-cards-canada); [Venn](https://www.venn.ca/resources/virtual-credit-cards-for-canadian-businesses-a-smarter-safer-way-to-spend); [stocktrades.ca](https://stocktrades.ca/virtual-credit-cards-canada)
- Card networks are building agent-specific rails: Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol and Mastercard's Agent Pay let merchants recognise registered agents acting for a cardholder — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/secure-agentic-commerce/)

### Inferences
- For Canadian consumers the "unique card per merchant" feature has no off-the-shelf wholesale provider confirmed in this research. A startup would have to become a prepaid program manager under a Canadian BIN sponsor (Peoples, Equitable or similar), run KYC on every user, hold/safeguard funds and register under both regimes. That is a regulated fintech build, not an API integration, and is the single largest cost and time item in the concept.
- Lower-cost alternatives: (a) launch without cards; (b) partner with an existing Canadian issuer (KOHO, Wealthsimple, Neo, EQ) if any will expose multi-card or merchant-lock controls; (c) launch cards US-first on Lithic/Stripe/Marqeta where consumer issuing is established.
- Card masking protects the card number and limits breach damage, but KYC means the issuer (and, through it, the guardian) knows the user's full identity; it is privacy from merchants, not anonymity.

### Gaps
- No evidence found either way on Lithic or Marqeta supporting Canadian consumer programs; Adyen Issuing geography was not checked.
- BIN-sponsorship commercial terms (setup fees, minimums, timelines) in Canada were not found.
- Whether KOHO/Wealthsimple/Neo/EQ allow multiple simultaneous or disposable virtual cards per user was not verified; sources are provider marketing and affiliate comparison sites.
- Whether Stripe Issuing Canada has moved from preview to general availability by November 2026 is unverified.

## 4. Verified attribute proofs / digital identity wallets (EUDI, mDL, Apple/Google Wallet, W3C VC, selective disclosure/ZKP, age-verification laws, Canada)

### Takeaway
The standards are finished and browsers now ship the plumbing, and age-verification law is the main adoption driver, but government-issued wallet credentials are live only in parts of the US and are mid-rollout in the EU. Ontario has nothing live: it announced consultations on 23 October 2026 with no launch dates. In Canada, "prove a fact without handing over the document" is not yet available as consumer infrastructure outside limited pilots and the bank-backed Interac Verified service.

### Cited Findings
EU
- Each member state must offer at least one EUDI wallet by 24 December 2026 (legal deadline); the Commission has said it doubts all will meet it, analysts expect a staggered launch, and fewer than one third of member states met a readiness benchmark (Politecnico di Milano observatory, cited secondarily) — [Trinsic](https://www.trinsic.id/blog/europe-s-digital-identity-deadline-is-real.-but-the-rollout-won-t-be-uniform); [Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/eudi-wallet-2026-deadline-rollout-eic-2026)
- As of September 2026: Italy, France and Finland named as frontrunners; Sweden's roadmap sets a 2028 or 2029 EUDI wallet release; Germany had not moved beyond prototypes — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/15/would-you-trust-one-app-with-all-your-digital-identity)
- A September 2026 tracker lists Austria, Greece, Luxembourg and Poland as having existing national apps being upgraded — [eID Easy](https://www.eideasy.com/blog/eu-digital-identity-wallets-september-2026)
- Regulated private sectors (e.g., banks, telecoms) must accept the wallet roughly a year after issuance begins, i.e., around late 2027 (forecast/legal target) — [Trinsic](https://www.trinsic.id/blog/europe-s-digital-identity-deadline-is-real.-but-the-rollout-won-t-be-uniform)
- The Commission declared its age-verification app technically ready on 11 May 2026; its April 2026 recommendation asks member states for plans by 30 June 2026 and at least one compliant solution per state by 31 December 2026 (target, not actual); it can be standalone or integrated into the EUDI wallet — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-urges-member-states-rollout-eu-age-verification-app); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/minors/eu-sets-the-clock-on-age-verification-rollout-urged-by-end%E2%80%912026/)
- The EU age-verification app uses zero-knowledge proofs so the relying party does not receive the date of birth — [Tech Policy Press](https://techpolicy.press/europes-age-verification-push-raises-privacy-issues-beyond-data-confidentiality)

United States and platform wallets
- As of August 2026, 14 US states offer driver's licences/IDs in Apple Wallet (Arkansas went live 27 May 2026), with North Carolina, Oklahoma, Utah and Virginia announced as coming — [MacRumors](https://macrumors.com/2026/08/17/apple-wallet-ids-expanding-to-four-more-states); [Technobezz](https://www.technobezz.com/news/apple-wallet-digital-ids-expand-to-four-more-states-including-north-carolina)
- Apple launched "Digital ID" built from a US passport in November 2025, usable at TSA checkpoints at 250+ airports, with Apple stating future use for identity and age verification in person, in apps and online; US passports only, and no expansion to other countries announced — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/11/12/apple-launches-digital-id-a-way-to-carry-your-passport-on-your-phone-for-use-at-tsa-checkpoints); [Biometric Update](https://www.biometricupdate.com/202511/apple-launches-digital-ids-based-on-us-passports-to-wallet)
- Google integrated zero-knowledge-proof age verification into Google Wallet (Bumble as launch partner) and open-sourced its ZKP libraries in July 2025 — [Google blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/platforms/google-pay/google-wallet-age-identity-verifications/); [Google blog (ZKP open-sourcing)](https://blog.google/technology/safety-security/opening-up-zero-knowledge-proof-technology-to-promote-privacy-in-age-assurance/)
- The W3C Digital Credentials API is enabled by default from Chrome 141, supported from Safari 26.0, and credential issuance ships by default in Chrome 155 (post dated 23 October 2026) — [Chrome developers: DC API shipped](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/digital-credentials-api-shipped); [Chrome developers: issuance](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/digital-credentials-api-issuance)
- The DC API is a W3C Working Draft in the Federated Identity Working Group; Safari support is mdoc-only; Mozilla's position is reported as negative even though baseline code landed in Firefox 149 (vendor blog) — [Corbado](https://www.corbado.com/blog/digital-credentials-api)

Standards
- OpenID for Verifiable Presentations 1.0 is final (July 2025) and defines use with the Digital Credentials API — [OpenID4VP 1.0](https://openid.net/specs/openid-4-verifiable-presentations-1_0.html); OpenID for Verifiable Credential Issuance 1.0 is final (September 2025) — [OpenID4VCI 1.0](https://openid.net/specs/openid-4-verifiable-credential-issuance-1_0.html)

### Inferences
- For an Ontario-based founder, selective-disclosure credentials cannot anchor a launch product: there is no Ontario wallet credential, no Canadian ID in Apple/Google Wallet found in this research, and provincial programs are pilots or health-card-only. The usable Canadian building blocks today are Interac Verified and conventional commercial ID verification, which verify identity but do not give the consumer a portable, minimal-disclosure credential.
- Wallet credentials are bound to the user's device and presented through the OS/browser (Digital Credentials API). A cloud-side guardian agent cannot present them remotely on the user's behalf; the guardian's role would be orchestration (telling the user when to present, choosing the minimal request), not holding the credential.
- The trajectory is favourable: standards finalized in 2025, browser support in 2025-26, and legal mandates (EU December 2026, age-verification laws) create relying-party demand. But merchant acceptance outside age checks, and Canada specifically, is a 2027+ story (forecast).

## 5. Personal data stores and user-held vaults (Solid/Inrupt, Digi.me, portability APIs, Sign in with Apple relay, passkeys, zero-knowledge vault architectures)

### Takeaway
User-held data stores have a decade of weak consumer adoption and offer no ready-made ecosystem to plug into, while zero-knowledge encrypted vaults (1Password-style) are mature and well documented. The hard design conflict for a guardian is that an autonomous agent needs to read the user's data to act, which is at odds with a vault the provider cannot read.

### Cited Findings
- Inrupt folded the inrupt.net pod service into Inrupt PodSpaces (running on its Enterprise Solid Server) in February 2025 — [Inrupt](https://www.inrupt.com/blog/inrupt-net-service-migration)
- A July 2026 ODI/EDM Council webinar Q&A (participant-submitted, not authoritative) states Solid "has struggled with adoption for 10 years now" and describes "Charlie", Inrupt's personal agentic-AI product built on Solid — [EDM Council Q&A PDF](https://edmcouncil.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/EDMWebinar_Solid-and-ODI_Q-and-A_20260714.pdf); the Open Data Institute is now involved in stewarding Solid — [ODI](https://theodi.org/news-and-events/blog/evolving-the-web-with-solid/)
- Digi.me was acquired by World Data Exchange in October 2022; no 2026 news on its status or user base was found — [World Data Exchange](https://worlddataexchange.umso.co/announcement)
- The EU Digital Markets Act obliges gatekeepers to provide data-portability tools — [Data Transfer Initiative](https://dtinit.org/blog/2024/04/29/supporting-effective-portability); a 2024 developer study found verification for portability API access prohibitively difficult, with only Google's Data Portability API working well among those reviewed — [MyData study](https://mydata.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Interoperability-and-the-DMA-in-Action-Developers-Experiences-of-Data-Portability-API-Access.pdf)
- Canada's consumer-driven (open) banking framework: proposed regulations published 27 June 2026 with consultation to 26 August 2026; launch timing is uncertain and a 2026 launch was widely viewed as at risk — [Bennett Jones](https://www.bennettjones.com/Insights/Blogs/2026/07/Canada-Advances-Consumer-Driven-Banking-Framework-with-Proposed-Regulations); [Open Banking Tracker](https://openbankingtracker.com/country/ca)
- Passkeys are mainstream: the FIDO Alliance estimates 5 billion passkeys in use worldwide (2026) — [FIDO Alliance](https://fidoalliance.org/fido-alliance-reports-accelerating-global-passkey-adoption-on-world-passkey-day-2026/)
- Sign in with Apple gives each app a distinct relay email address (privaterelay.appleid.com) — [Spam Resource](https://www.spamresource.com/2023/08/private-relay-vs-private-relay-vs-hide.html)
- 1Password's security design white paper (release 0.5.2, 22 January 2026): all keys are generated on the client, all encryption is local, unlocking needs both the account password and a device-held Secret Key that 1Password never sees, and authentication uses SRP — [1Password Security Design white paper](https://agilebits.github.io/security-design/1Password_Security_Design.pdf); [1Password blog](https://1password.com/blog/the-architectural-reason-1password-cant-read-your-vault-data/)
- A 2026 ETH Zurich paper tested password managers' zero-knowledge claims against a fully malicious server; 1Password acknowledged a documented limitation that users have no robust way to verify that a public key they encrypt to belongs to the intended recipient — [1Password blog](https://1password.com/blog/eth-zurich-zero-knowledge-malicious-server-review)

### Inferences
- Solid/Inrupt and Digi.me are not viable distribution or integration platforms for a consumer startup: companies do not read from or write to consumer pods at scale. The guardian would be building its own vault.
- A zero-knowledge vault protects stored data, but a server-side AI agent that drafts requests, reads mail and fills forms must see plaintext at the time it acts. The realistic options are: on-device agent execution, short-lived just-in-time decryption inside hardened/confidential compute, or accepting that the provider can technically access data while minimizing what is retained. None gives the clean "we cannot read your data" claim that password managers make. This trade-off should be stated honestly in the product's claims.
- Passkeys reduce the value of the guardian storing passwords, but passkeys are device/ecosystem-bound, which limits what a cloud agent can log into on the user's behalf.

### Gaps
- Proton's zero-access encryption and Bitwarden's open-source audit documentation were not retrieved.
- Confidential-computing/TEE options for running LLM agents over encrypted personal data were not researched.
- No adoption metrics for Inrupt PodSpaces or Inrupt's Charlie.

## 6. Leak detection and attribution (breach feeds, data-broker scanning, canaries, credit-bureau monitoring, reliability of per-company aliases)

### Takeaway
Breach-exposure data is purchasable (Have I Been Pwned from about US$4.39/month; richer commercial feeds are custom-priced) and credit freezes are newly available in Ontario. Per-company aliases reliably show which relationship an address escaped through, but cannot distinguish a breach from a sale, permitted sharing, or a compromise at the company's vendor, and they attribute only the aliased identifier.

### Cited Findings
- Have I Been Pwned replaced its Pwned 1-5/Ultra plans in late March 2026 with Core, Pro and High RPM tiers — [HIBP support](https://support.haveibeenpwned.com/hc/en-au/articles/15617510034063-What-happened-to-the-old-Pwned-subscription-plans); pricing starts at US$4.39/month (Core), US$379/month (Pro) and US$1,150/month (High RPM), billed annually — [HIBP subscription page](https://haveibeenpwned.com/subscription)
- Stealer-log data is not available on Core; Pro and above can retrieve the website domains where an email address was captured in stealer logs; domain searches require the domain to be verified — [HIBP support](https://support.haveibeenpwned.com/hc/en-au/articles/12029246540175-What-stealer-log-information-can-I-access-with-a-Pro-or-High-RPM-subscription); [HIBP API v3](https://haveibeenpwned.com/API/v3)
- Flare (the Canadian vendor named in the brief) offers embedded/API integration with volume-based pricing and a white-label MSSP program; its tiers are custom-priced — [Flare embedded solutions](https://flare.io/embedded-solutions); [Flare MSSP](https://flare.io/mssp); [FitGap profile](https://us.fitgap.com/products/020740/flare)
- SpyCloud positions itself as an identity-threat-protection platform (self-published comparison) — [SpyCloud](https://spycloud.com/competitors/spycloud-vs-flare/); a sponsored-style guide puts dark-web-monitoring pricing at roughly US$1,000-2,500/month mid-market and US$5,000-20,000/month enterprise (category estimate, low reliability) — [Decryption Digest](https://www.decryptiondigest.com/blog/dark-web-monitoring-service-cost-pricing-guide)
- Unique-address attribution is an established research technique: UC San Diego's "Tripwire" (2017) registered accounts with a unique email address each and determined that 19 websites had been compromised — [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2017-12-scientists-simple-tool-websites-breach.pdf)
- Aliases were present in only about 0.03% of records in the 2013 Adobe breach, and seeing an alias in a leaked dataset can make it trivial to identify which company leaked — [Krebs on Security](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2022/08/the-security-pros-and-cons-of-using-email-aliases/)
- A 2020 IEEE EuroS&P workshop study using separated email addresses found some services pass addresses to other providers but disclose this in their privacy policies — [IEEE EuroS&PW 2020 paper](https://conferences.computer.org/eurosp/pdfs/EuroSPW2020-7k9FlVRX4z43j4uE2SeXU0/859700a645/859700a645.pdf)
- Canadian credit bureaus: Borrowell draws on Equifax and Credit Karma Canada on TransUnion; the bureaus' own paid monitoring runs about C$19.95-24.95/month (affiliate-site figures) — [WealthNorth](https://wealthnorth.ca/credit-cards/credit-score/equifax-vs-transunion-canada/); TransUnion Canada markets credit, identity and fraud solutions to fintechs — [TransUnion Canada](https://www.transunion.ca/industry/fintech)
- Credit freezes in Canada: Quebec has required free freezes from Equifax and TransUnion since February 2023; Ontario's became available 1 July 2026 (O. Reg. 168/25 under Bill 142), with Equifax live and TransUnion given until 1 July 2027; no other province has a freeze law in force — [Equifax Canada](https://www.equifax.ca/about-equifax/newsroom/-/intlpress/equifax-canada-introduces-credit-lock-to-empower-ontarians-and-help-fight-identity-theft-and-fraud/); [Credit Karma Canada](https://www.creditkarma.ca/credit/i/credit-freezes-expanding-in-canada); [Loans Canada](https://loanscanada.ca/credit/can-you-get-a-credit-freeze-in-canada/)
- Data-broker scanning/removal vendors cover Canada to varying degrees: Optery is available to Canadian residents excluding Quebec — [Optery](https://www.optery.com/optery-is-officially-available-for-residents-of-canada/); Incogni and DeleteMe list Canada as supported — [NordVPN comparison](https://nordvpn.com/blog/incogni-vs-deleteme/)

### Inferences
- What a per-company alias proves: mail arriving at alias X from anyone other than company X means the address left company X's control. What it does not prove: whether that was a breach, a sale, disclosed sharing with partners, or a compromise of X's email/CRM vendor. It also says nothing about other data fields (name, address, card) and nothing if the leaked data is never used to contact the user. "Attributes which company leaked" should be marketed as "identifies which relationship the address escaped through".
- Attribution requires unguessable aliases (random strings, not company-name@userdomain) and a classifier to separate the company's own mail and its legitimate processors from third parties; false accusations against companies are a product and legal risk.
- Combining alias signals with breach feeds improves confidence: an alias appearing in a named breach corpus is far stronger evidence than spam alone. HIBP Core is affordable for a startup; stealer-log and commercial feeds push costs into hundreds to thousands of US dollars per month before per-user fees.
- Ontario's July 2026 freeze right gives a Canada-based guardian a concrete protective action to automate after a leak, though whether the bureaus offer APIs for third parties to place freezes is unknown.

### Gaps
- No public API/partner terms or pricing found for Equifax Canada, TransUnion Canada, SpyCloud, Constella or Recorded Future consumer-monitoring feeds.
- Canary/honeytoken services for individuals (e.g., Thinkst Canarytokens) were not researched this session; their existence is background knowledge, not verified.
- No study quantifying false-positive/false-negative rates of alias-based attribution was found.
- The Canadian data-broker/people-search landscape, and why Optery excludes Quebec, were not researched.

## 7. Automating data-rights requests (GPC, IAB DDRF, California DROP, Data Rights Protocol, discovery via inbox scanning, CASA)

### Takeaway
The machine-readable rights infrastructure (DROP, mandated opt-out signals, the Data Rights Protocol) is real but is built on California and other US state law; none of it gives a Canadian resident enforceable leverage. Outside those channels requests still go by email and web form, where measured broker response rates are low, and discovering who holds a user's data by scanning their inbox triggers Google's annual restricted-scope security assessment.

### Cited Findings
- California's DROP sends one standardized deletion/opt-out request to all data brokers registered with the state — [CPPA](https://cppa.ca.gov/data_brokers); brokers had to begin retrieving requests from 1 August 2026, must check at least every 45 days and process within 45 days — [privacy.ca.gov](https://privacy.ca.gov/drop-for-data-brokers/); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2026/08/californians-can-tell-data-brokers-to-drop-their-information)
- Early DROP figures (secondary, citing IAPP coverage of the CPPA's August board meeting; unverified): roughly 450,000 consumer deletion requests submitted, and only about 30% of registered brokers had begun processing in the first week of enforceability — [Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/california-drop-compliance-data-brokers)
- Penalties for brokers are described as US$200 per request per day — [Clym](https://www.clym.io/blog/california-drop-compliance-data-brokers); TrustArc describes a US$100-200 range — [TrustArc](https://trustarc.com/resource/california-delete-act-drop-platform-data-brokers/)
- California announced a data-broker enforcement action against LocateSmarter on 13 August 2026 — [Office of the Governor of California](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/08/13/icymi-california-takes-historic-action-against-data-brokers/)
- California's 603 registered data brokers received 58 million deletion requests in 2024 and denied 890,712 — [Control D report](https://controld.com/blog/data-broker-report/)
- Opt-out signals: California's AB 566 (signed 8 October 2025, effective 1 January 2027) requires browsers to include an opt-out preference signal setting; it does not name GPC and need not be on by default — [privacy.ca.gov](https://privacy.ca.gov/2026/01/californias-opt-me-out-act-your-privacy-just-got-easier); [TrueVault](https://www.truevault.com/learn/new-ca-law-mandates-support-for-opt-out-signals); Colorado and Connecticut also require honouring approved signals including GPC, and the three states ran a joint compliance investigation — [Didomi](https://didomi.io/blog/global-privacy-control-gpc-2026)
- IAB Tech Lab finalized its Data Deletion Request Framework in mid-2024 (cryptographically signed deletion signals through the ad supply chain) and later released a version 2; no adoption figures were found — [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/iab-tech-lab-finalizes-data-deletion-request-framework-to-streamline-digital-ad-supply-chain-privacy-compliance-302163770.html); [Captain Compliance](https://captaincompliance.com/education/iab-tech-lab-advances-privacy-infrastructure-with-gpp-refresh-and-ddrf-version-2/)
- The Data Rights Protocol is a technical standard for exchanging CCPA data-rights requests, developed by a consortium of Consumer Reports and nine organizations; authorized-agent implementers include Permission Slip, Yorba, Incogni and Mine; requests are authenticated with public-key cryptography — [CR Innovation Lab: DRP](https://innovation.consumerreports.org/initiatives/data-rights-protocol/); [CR Innovation Lab: securing the protocol](https://innovation.consumerreports.org/securing-a-common-protocol-for-consumer-data-rights-requests)
- Consumer Reports and OneTrust completed end-to-end DRP testing in production; no 2026 adoption counts were found — [CR Innovation Lab](https://innovation.consumerreports.org/first-end-to-end-test-of-draft-data-rights-protocol-completed-successfully)
- Discovery: Mine asks permission to scan email subject lines in the user's inbox to infer which companies probably hold their data — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/privacy/authorized-agents-plan-to-make-it-easier-to-delete-your-data-a8655835448); [Chrome Unboxed](https://chromeunboxed.com/say-mine-app-reclaim-your-digital-footprint)
- Consumer Reports' study of authorized agents found the participant experience "disorienting and exasperating", and Incogni reported brokers that had honoured requests for months starting to reject new ones — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/privacy/authorized-agents-plan-to-make-it-easier-to-delete-your-data-a8655835448)
- A July 2026 academic study ("Let My Data Go", arXiv 2607.04552) sent CCPA opt-out and deletion requests to all California-registered brokers in fall 2025 and reports a 26-38% response rate, significant non-response and CCPA-disallowed identity-verification demands — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04552); press coverage says most brokers let the 45-day window lapse without reporting an outcome — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/07/10/trouble-with-data-broker-deletion-requests/)
- A 2024 PoPETs paper on people-search sites found identity-verification issues complicate removal — [NSF public access copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10549421)
- Gmail inbox access: restricted Gmail scopes require a CASA Tier 2 security assessment that renews annually; send-only (gmail.send) is a sensitive, not restricted, scope — [Nango docs](https://nango.dev/docs/api-integrations/google-shared/google-security-review); [Unipile](https://www.unipile.com/integrating-google-oauth-2-0-user-authentication-into-your-app/)
- CASA Tier 2 cost reports vary widely: about US$540/year via TAC Security at a Google-negotiated rate (community-reported) — [Google Apps Script community thread](https://groups.google.com/g/google-apps-script-community/c/XgsHT2Fr7u8); US$3,000-4,500 packages from Leviathan Security — [Leviathan Security](https://leviathansecurity.com/programs/google-casa-cloud-application-security-assessment); US$15,000-75,000 claimed by a vendor selling an alternative (interested party) — [inbox-dog blog](https://inbox-dog.pages.dev/blog/casa-tier-2-gmail-oauth)

### Inferences
- For a Canadian consumer product, DROP, GPC mandates and the DRP are useful only for users with California/US-state rights or where companies voluntarily extend them. In Canada the agent falls back to emailing privacy officers and filling forms, with no statutory authorized-agent mechanism identified in this research.
- The measured 26-38% broker response rate under the strongest US regime (with real penalties) is a realistic ceiling for what automation alone achieves; value comes from persistence, follow-up and escalation, which is labour that an AI agent can plausibly do cheaply.
- Inbox scanning is the only proven discovery method, and it is also the product's largest privacy and security liability. If the guardian issues the aliases itself, it already knows every company the user dealt with from sign-up onward, so inbox access is needed only for legacy accounts and could be a one-time, on-device or metadata-only import.
- CASA's direct fee is modest at the low end; the real costs are engineering to pass it, the annual renewal, Google's limited-use policy constraints on what the data may be used for, and equivalent reviews for Microsoft/Apple mail.

### Gaps
- Google's own restricted-scope and CASA pages could not be fetched; CASA requirements and costs rest on third-party reports and need primary confirmation, including how Google's limited-use policy treats LLM processing of mail content.
- No DRP or IAB DDRF adoption numbers for 2026.
- DROP's consumer-side launch date and current request totals were not confirmed from the CPPA directly; the 450,000/30% figures are secondary.
- Canadian law on data-rights requests made by an agent (PIPEDA, Quebec Law 25, any pending federal bill) was outside this stream and not researched; treat the statement that Canada lacks an authorized-agent regime as unverified background.
- How DeleteMe discovers data holders (broker-list scanning vs. inbox) was not sourced.

## 8. AI agents acting for consumers (browser/computer-use agents, agent identity standards, prompt injection, how companies treat bots)

### Takeaway
Agents can now operate browsers and email at roughly human benchmark level, so the "do the paperwork" labour is technically feasible. Two problems remain unsolved: prompt injection when the agent reads untrusted email or web content, and the absence of any standard way for an agent to prove it is authorized by a specific person, which leaves it exposed to CAPTCHAs, identity checks and site policies.

### Cited Findings
- Capability: top computer-use agents score about 85-86% on OSWorld-Verified against a human baseline of 72.36%; sources conflict on the current leader, and scores depend on scaffolding and tool access (third-party leaderboards, not the official one) — [BenchLM](https://www.benchlm.ai/benchmarks/osworld-verified); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/computer-use-agents-85-percent-osworld-benchmark/)
- Commentary cautions that passing the benchmark's human baseline is not evidence of dependable real-world task completion, and recommends pairing scores with workflow trials — [BuildMVPFast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/osworld-benchmark-ai-agents-passed-human-baseline-2026)
- Prompt injection: VPI-Bench (ICLR 2026; 306 test cases across Amazon, Booking, BBC, Messenger and Email) found browser-use agents attempted the injected action in up to 100% of cases on some platforms and a computer-use agent in up to 59%; a benign email-reply task paired with a malicious email reached a 96.67% attempted rate; system-prompt defences gave no significant improvement — [arXiv 2506.02456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02456v2)
- A secondary report of an Anthropic system card gives a browser agent a 31.5% raw hijack rate, falling to 0.5% with built-in safeguards (single secondary source; verify against the system card) — [pulse.bot citing VentureBeat](https://www.pulse.bot/ai/news/anthropics-browser-agent-got-hijacked-315-of-the-time-before-safeguards-engaged-08ce0929-21bd-42f1-ab59-1f78400b4f7f/)
- Real-world disclosures in 2026: Zenity Labs' "PleaseFix" zero-click hijack family affecting Perplexity Comet and other agentic browsers (March 2026) — [Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-pleasefix-agentic-browser-exploits-2026032/); LayerX's "BioShocking" research compromising six AI browsers/extensions for credential theft (June 2026) — [Cloud Security Alliance](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-ai-browser-prompt-injection-20260630-csa-s/)
- Agent identity: the IETF Web Bot Auth working-group draft (revision -00 September 2026, -01 October 2026) lets agents sign HTTP requests and publish keys at an HTTPS URL; the draft states it says nothing about who operates the agent, whether it is benign, or whether the request is authorized — [IETF datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol)
- Visa's Trusted Agent Protocol and Mastercard's Agent Pay use Web Bot Auth as the agent-authentication layer, with each network hosting a directory of registered agents; signatures can assert an agent is acting for a specific cardholder — [Cloudflare blog](https://blog.cloudflare.com/secure-agentic-commerce/); [Cloudflare press release](https://www.cloudflare.com/press/press-releases/2025/cloudflare-collaborates-with-leading-payments-companies-to-secure-and-enable-agentic-commerce/)
- The OpenID Foundation's October 2025 whitepaper finds OAuth/OpenID Connect handle simple single-organization agent cases but leave gaps for cross-organization, autonomous and delegated agent action — [OpenID Foundation](https://openid.net/new-whitepaper-tackles-ai-agent-identity-challenges/); [arXiv 2510.25819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25819v1)
- Detection: Chromium-based agents present genuine browser fingerprints, so header inspection cannot distinguish them from human users — [SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/five-attack-categories-every-security-team-must-understand)
- Legal treatment: a US district court enjoined Perplexity's Comet agent from accessing password-protected Amazon accounts (9 March 2026), finding it acted with the user's permission but without Amazon's authorization — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexitys-ai-shopping-bots); the Ninth Circuit vacated the injunction in August 2026, reasoning that when a user directs the assistant the user is the one accessing Amazon, while limiting the holding to the record before it — [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/newsletters/us-court-overturns-amazon-injunction-perplexity); [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/perplexity-comet-amazon-ai-shopping-agents-ruling)
- Amazon updated its Business Solutions Agreement (effective 4 March 2026) to require AI agents to identify themselves — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/court-blocks-perplexitys-comet-browser-from-amazons-accounts/)
- Companies add friction to agent-submitted rights requests through identity-verification demands, including ones the CCPA disallows — [arXiv 2607.04552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04552)

### Inferences
- The guardian's core loop (read inbound mail to aliases, decide, act) is exactly the configuration where prompt injection is most dangerous: untrusted content, access to private data and the ability to act. Because every company and spammer can email the user's aliases, attackers get a direct channel to the agent. Mitigations are architectural (separating the component that reads mail from the component that can act, narrow allow-listed actions, human approval for irreversible steps such as rotating details or submitting ID), not a model setting.
- Agent identity standards currently prove "this is agent software from operator X" (Web Bot Auth), and in payments "registered with Visa/Mastercard". Nothing standard proves "this agent is authorized by this named person to exercise their privacy rights"; in practice that is still a signed authorization letter or power of attorney plus the company's own identity check.
- Sending and following up on rights-request emails is far more robust than driving third-party web forms, which face CAPTCHAs, bot detection and terms-of-service prohibitions. A product plan should assume email-first automation with human fallback for a material share of web flows.
- The legal status of user-directed agents logging into third-party accounts is unsettled (one US appellate ruling limited to its facts; no Canadian authority found).

### Gaps
- No data on agent success rates specifically for privacy-request forms, CAPTCHA encounter rates or account lockouts.
- MCP authorization, OAuth-based delegation drafts for agents and any 2026 OpenID agent-identity profiles were not retrieved.
- Official OSWorld leaderboard and vendor system cards were not checked directly.
- Per-task model cost for running such an agent continuously was not researched.

## 9. Security architecture risks (the guardian as honeypot; lessons from password-manager and identity-protection breaches)

### Takeaway
A guardian concentrates identity mappings, mail flow, payment credentials and (if it scans inboxes) correspondence in one place, making it a higher-value target than a password manager. Precedent shows such vendors do get breached, through employees and backups rather than broken cryptography, with losses surfacing years later.

### Cited Findings
- LastPass 2022: the chain began with a compromised developer laptop; an employee's reuse of one master password across personal and business vaults let the attacker reach an AWS access key and decryption key and steal encrypted vault backups — [Wikipedia: 2022 LastPass data breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_LastPass_data_breach)
- Stolen vault backups can be attacked offline, with outcomes depending on master-password strength and encryption settings; a US$150 million theft in 2025 was linked to the 2022 data, and Brian Krebs described "a steady trickle of six-figure cryptocurrency heists" tied to it — [Wikipedia: 2022 LastPass data breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_LastPass_data_breach)
- The UK ICO fined LastPass UK Ltd GBP 1.2 million over the breach, which affected up to 1.6 million UK users, for failing to implement sufficiently robust technical and security measures — [The Record](https://therecord.media/uk-fines-lastpass-over-1-million-data-breach); [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ico-fines-lastpass-12m-2022-breach)
- LastPass settled a class action for US$24.5 million in 2025; the ICO reportedly found no evidence of successful vault decryption, which conflicts with attribution of the thefts to cracked vaults — [Wikipedia: 2022 LastPass data breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_LastPass_data_breach)
- Identity-protection vendor incident: Aura confirmed in March 2026 a breach of roughly 900,000 contact records after an employee fell for voice phishing; it said about 35,000 were customers (another report: under 20,000 current and under 15,000 former), the rest a marketing list acquired in 2021, and that SSNs, passwords and financial data were not exposed — [IdentityIQ](https://www.identityiq.com/articles/aura-data-breach-2026); [Privacy Guides breach roundup](https://www.privacyguides.org/news/2026/03/20/data-breach-roundup-mar-13-19-2026.md)
- Identity-verification vendors are also being breached: IDScan disclosed unauthorized access on 4 September 2026, with a filing citing 13 million people affected against an unverified seller claim of 153 million records — [eSecurity Planet](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/news/news-idscan-data-breach-13-million-people/)
- Reference architecture: client-side key generation and encryption, a two-secret design (password plus device-held Secret Key) so stolen server data resists offline cracking, and a published design white paper and third-party audit — [1Password Security Design white paper](https://agilebits.github.io/security-design/1Password_Security_Design.pdf)
- Even well-designed zero-knowledge systems have residual malicious-server weaknesses (e.g., unverified public keys), per 2026 ETH Zurich research — [1Password blog](https://1password.com/blog/eth-zurich-zero-knowledge-malicious-server-review)

### Inferences
- Lessons that transfer directly: (1) encrypted-at-rest backups will eventually be stolen, so resistance to offline cracking must not depend on user-chosen password strength (the 1Password two-secret model addresses the LastPass failure mode); (2) breaches start with staff devices and social engineering, so internal access to production secrets matters more than cipher choice; (3) unencrypted metadata is itself sensitive, and for a guardian the alias-to-identity map and the list of companies a person deals with is the crown-jewel dataset.
- The guardian's exposure is larger than a password manager's in three ways: it must hold or access plaintext to act (section 5), it sits in the live mail/phone/payment path so a compromise enables interception and account takeover in real time, and its agent can be manipulated from outside via prompt injection (section 8).
- Regulatory and liability exposure is material for a small company (LastPass: regulator fine plus a US$24.5m settlement). Minimizing retained data, per-user keys, on-device processing where possible, and hard limits on what the agent may do without user confirmation are architecture decisions that need to precede product build.

### Gaps
- No public post-mortems found for breaches at DeleteMe, Incogni or Optery (none surfaced; absence of results is not proof of absence).
- The 2023 Norton LifeLock credential-stuffing incident and similar cases were not sourced this session.
- Cyber-insurance availability and cost for this risk profile, and Canadian breach-notification obligations, were not researched.
- Figures above US$400 million for LastPass-linked thefts appear only in a secondary compliance blog and are unverified.

## 10. Feasibility summary: what is feasible today, what depends on infrastructure still rolling out, what is not feasible

### Takeaway
A Canada-based startup can build the alias, monitoring and request-automation core today; payment cards and verified-attribute proofs depend on infrastructure that is not yet available to Canadian consumers; and a fully autonomous, provider-blind, definitively attributing guardian is not achievable with current technology. This section is the researcher's synthesis of sections 1-9, and each line is an inference resting on the findings cited there.

### Cited Findings
- Email aliasing is open source, self-hostable and priced at about US$1-5 per user per month at retail — [SimpleLogin blog](https://simplelogin.io/blog/vs-firefox-relay/); [emcognito comparison](https://emcognito.com/compare)
- Programmable phone numbers cost about US$1 per number per month, and VoIP numbers are refused for verification by banks and major platforms — [Telnyx pricing](https://telnyx.com/pricing/numbers.md); [Zuper docs](https://docs.zuper.co/Zuper_Connect/OTP/2FA%20Verification%20Codes)
- Merchant-locked consumer cards are US-only (Privacy.com); Stripe Issuing in Canada is a business-only preview — [Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/privacy-dot-com-alternatives); [Stripe Canada terms](https://stripe.com/legal/issuing/charge-card-accountholder-terms-ca)
- Ontario announced digital-ID consultations on 23 October 2026 with no launch dates; the EU wallet deadline is 24 December 2026 with uneven readiness — [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/politics/queens-park/2026/10/23/ontario-plans-to-launch-digital-ids-including-drivers-licences-and-health-cards/); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/09/15/would-you-trust-one-app-with-all-your-digital-identity)
- Breach-exposure lookups start at US$4.39/month, and Ontario credit freezes became available 1 July 2026 — [HIBP subscription page](https://haveibeenpwned.com/subscription); [Equifax Canada](https://www.equifax.ca/about-equifax/newsroom/-/intlpress/equifax-canada-introduces-credit-lock-to-empower-ontarians-and-help-fight-identity-theft-and-fraud/)
- Data brokers responded to only 26-38% of CCPA requests in a 2025 field study — [arXiv 2607.04552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04552)
- Email-reading agents followed injected instructions in up to 96.67% of test cases in one benchmark scenario, and the IETF agent-authentication draft does not establish user authorization — [arXiv 2506.02456](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.02456v2); [IETF datatracker](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-ietf-webbotauth-httpsig-protocol)

### Inferences
Feasible today (including in Canada)
- Unique email alias per company, with forwarding, reply-through, disable and rotate, on self-hosted or open-source software. Expect a minority of sites to reject alias domains.
- A small number of masked phone numbers per user for non-critical contact fields (not one per company; not for bank or major-platform 2FA).
- Breach and exposure monitoring via HIBP and commercial feeds (Flare and peers), plus data-broker scanning through existing vendors with partial Canadian coverage.
- Alias-based leak detection framed as "which relationship this address escaped through", strengthened by matching against breach corpora.
- Drafting, sending, tracking and following up data-rights requests by email with an LLM agent, with a human-approval step for consequential actions.
- An encrypted vault using a published client-side, two-secret key design for data at rest.
- Automating credit-freeze guidance for Ontario and Quebec residents (whether via API or user-guided steps is unknown).

Depends on infrastructure still rolling out
- Disposable or merchant-locked cards for Canadian consumers: needs a BIN-sponsor program and dual MSB/RPAA registration, or a partner issuer, or a wholesale consumer-issuing product reaching Canada (Stripe Issuing Canada is business-only preview; no Lithic/Marqeta Canada evidence found).
- Verified-attribute proofs (age, residency, licence status without revealing the document): US state mDLs and Apple/Google wallets are live in parts of the US; EU wallets are due from December 2026 with private-sector acceptance about a year later (target); Ontario is at consultation stage with no dates; BC and Alberta are limited to pilots or a health card; Quebec has enabling law but no launch date found.
- Standardized agent identity and delegated authority: Web Bot Auth is an IETF working-group draft; Visa/Mastercard agent protocols cover payments only; no standard yet proves an agent is authorized by a specific person.
- One-click, legally backed bulk deletion (DROP), mandated opt-out signals and the Data Rights Protocol: operating or scheduled, but for California/US-state residents; no Canadian equivalent identified.
- Consumer-driven banking and bank-data portability in Canada: regulations proposed June 2026; live date uncertain.

Not feasible, or not honestly claimable, with current technology
- Proving that a specific company was breached, as opposed to showing the alias given to it reached third parties; attributing leaks of data other than the aliased identifiers.
- Phone-number attribution at per-company granularity at consumer price points, and using masked VoIP numbers as the verification number for banks and major platforms.
- Fully hands-off autonomy when the agent reads untrusted mail or web content: prompt injection has no complete defence, so high-stakes actions need human confirmation.
- A true "we cannot read your data" guarantee combined with a server-side agent that acts on that data; one of the two has to give.
- Guaranteed deletion or guaranteed compliance by data holders: outcomes depend on companies' cooperation, and measured response rates are low even where penalties exist.
- Reliable unattended completion of arbitrary third-party web forms and logged-in account actions, given CAPTCHAs, identity checks, site terms and unsettled law on agent access.
- Rotating identifiers that are not replaceable (name, date of birth, SIN, home address); rotation applies only to the stand-ins the guardian issued.

### Gaps
- No cost model was built; unit economics need wholesale quotes (phone numbers, breach feeds, card program, model inference) that were not obtainable from public pages in this session.
- Feasibility judgements about Canadian card issuing rest on absence of evidence for Lithic/Marqeta/Adyen consumer programs in Canada rather than on confirmation from those providers.
- All findings come from search-result summaries because page fetches were blocked; primary-source verification is recommended for every figure the final report relies on, especially HIBP pricing, Stripe Issuing Canada status, DROP statistics, CASA costs and the Ontario digital-ID announcement.
